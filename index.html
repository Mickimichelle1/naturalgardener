All-Natural Pest Control Recipes

🧄 Spray Recipe

Garlic Pepper Spray

A powerful deterrent for soft-bodied insects like aphids, spider mites, and caterpillars — safe for plants and pets.

🧪 Ingredients
  • 1 whole bulb of garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 tsp dish soap (castile)
  • 1 quart warm water

📋 Instructions Blend garlic with ½ cup water. Strain into a quart jar. Add cayenne pepper and dish soap. Dilute with remaining water. Spray directly on affected plants in the early morning. Reapply after rain or every 7–10 days.
🌿 Spray Recipe

Neem Oil Fungal & Insect Spray

Tackles aphids, whitefly, powdery mildew, and fungal issues — a homestead staple for the entire growing season.

🧪 Ingredients
  • 2 tbsp cold-pressed neem oil
  • 1 tsp castile soap
  • 1 quart warm water

📋 Instructions Mix soap into warm water first, then add neem oil. Shake well before each use. Spray all leaf surfaces every 7–14 days. Avoid applying in direct afternoon sun.
🪨 Barrier Method

Diatomaceous Earth Barrier

Non-toxic fossilized powder that destroys the exoskeletons of slugs, beetles, earwigs, and ants on contact.

🧪 What You Need
  • Food-grade diatomaceous earth (DE)
  • A duster or old flour shaker

📋 Instructions Apply a thin ring of DE around the base of vulnerable plants or along garden bed edges. Reapply after every rain or heavy dew. Always use food-grade DE and avoid inhaling the dust.
🫧 Spray Recipe

Insecticidal Soap Spray

A gentle yet effective contact spray for scale insects, mealybugs, and aphids — works by suffocating the pest.

🧪 Ingredients
  • 1.5 tsp pure castile soap (unscented)
  • 1 quart cool water
  • Optional: 5 drops peppermint essential oil

📋 Instructions Mix soap and water gently. Spray directly onto pests. Works best in early morning or evening. Test on a single leaf 24 hrs before broad application.
🍶 Weed Control

Vinegar Weed Killer

A fast-acting natural herbicide for paths, driveways, and cracks — kills weeds without harsh chemicals.

🧪 Ingredients
  • 1 gallon white or horticultural vinegar (20% acidity)
  • 1 cup salt
  • 1 tbsp dish soap

📋 Instructions Combine and stir until salt dissolves. Spray directly on weed foliage on a sunny day. Non-selective — keep away from garden beds and lawn.
🌻 Prevention

Companion Planting Overview

Strategically planting pest-repelling herbs and flowers is the oldest natural pest control method in the world.

🌱 Top Companion Pairings
  • Basil next to tomatoes — repels aphids & hornworms
  • Marigolds around the garden edge — deters nematodes & whitefly
  • Lavender near brassicas — repels cabbage moths
  • Nasturtiums as trap crops — aphids prefer them
  • Dill & fennel attract beneficial predatory wasps

Backyard Poultry Care

🏡 Housing

Setting Up the Perfect Coop

A safe, clean, and comfortable coop is the foundation of healthy backyard chickens. Here's how to get it right.

📐 Key Coop Requirements
  • Minimum 4 sq ft per bird inside the coop
  • At least 10 sq ft per bird in the run
  • 1 nesting box per 3–4 hens, filled with straw
  • Roosting bars set 12–18" off the floor
  • Good ventilation — vents near the roof, not drafts
  • Predator-proof latches (raccoons open simple hooks)
🌾 Feeding

Natural Feeding & Nutrition

Laying hens need a balanced diet. Supplement store-bought feed with forage and kitchen scraps for peak health.

🥣 What to Feed
  • 16–18% protein layer pellets or crumbles as the base
  • Oyster shell free-choice for strong eggshells
  • Kitchen scraps: veggies, fruit, cooked grains, yogurt
  • Grit (coarse sand/granite) for digestion
  • Fermented feed — soak 48–72 hrs for gut health

🚫 Avoid:
  • Avocado, chocolate, onion, raw potato, citrus
🦆 Ducks

Raising Backyard Ducks

Ducks are excellent for pest control, provide rich eggs, and are surprisingly cold-hardy. A great addition to any homestead.

🌊 Duck Essentials
  • Always provide deep water for dunking heads
  • Kiddie pools work great as a low-cost pond substitute
  • Best beginner breeds: Pekin, Khaki Campbell, Welsh Harlequin
  • Ducks don't need a coop — a simple 3-sided shelter is fine
  • They'll eat slugs, snails & insects across your garden
💊 Natural Health

Natural Parasite Prevention

Keep your flock free of lice, mites, and intestinal worms using simple, all-natural remedies and preventive habits.

🌿 Natural Treatments
  • Dust bath with wood ash & DE — prevents lice & mites
  • Apple cider vinegar (1 tbsp/gallon water) — boosts gut health
  • Garlic powder in feed — natural wormer
  • Pumpkin seeds — traditional dewormer, fed raw
  • Rotate pasture areas to break parasite cycles
🥚 Egg Production

Maximizing Natural Egg Laying

Understand what drives egg production and how to support your hens through winter slumps naturally.

☀️ Laying Factors
  • Hens need 14–16 hrs of light to lay consistently
  • Add a low-watt bulb on a timer for winter supplementation
  • Stress (predators, overcrowding) halts laying quickly
  • Hens molt 1x per year — no eggs during molt is normal
  • Best laying breeds: ISA Brown, Rhode Island Red, Leghorn
🐦 Quail

Raising Coturnix Quail

Quail are ideal for small homesteads — they lay at 6–8 weeks old, are quiet, and need minimal space.

🐣 Quail Basics
  • 1 sq ft per bird in a raised wire cage or hutch
  • Reach laying age in just 6–8 weeks
  • Lay 250–300 eggs per year
  • Feed 20–24% protein game bird feed
  • Keep 1 male per 3–5 females
  • Quiet — no crowing, perfect for suburban backyards

Growing Perennial Flowers

💜 Perennial

Lavender

Aromatic, drought-tolerant, and irresistible to bees & butterflies. Also doubles as a natural pest repellent.

🌱 Growing Guide
  • Hardiness: Zones 5–9
  • Sun: Full sun (6+ hours)
  • Soil: Well-drained, slightly alkaline
  • Watering: Drought-tolerant once established
  • Pruning: Cut back ⅓ after first bloom flush
  • Best variety: English lavender (most cold-hardy)
🌸 Perennial

Echinacea (Coneflower)

A stunning prairie native beloved by bees and goldfinches. Low-maintenance, deer-resistant, and medicinal.

🌱 Growing Guide
  • Hardiness: Zones 3–9
  • Sun: Full sun to part shade
  • Soil: Tolerates poor, dry soils
  • Bloom time: June through October
  • Leave seedheads over winter — birds love the seeds
  • Divide every 3–4 years to keep clumps vigorous
🌼 Perennial

Black-Eyed Susan

A cheerful, rugged native wildflower that thrives in difficult conditions and self-seeds freely around the garden.

🌱 Growing Guide
  • Hardiness: Zones 3–10
  • Sun: Full sun preferred
  • Soil: Adaptable — tolerates clay, sandy, and dry soils
  • Bloom time: July through September
  • Self-seeds readily — thin seedlings in spring
  • Great for cutting gardens, naturalized meadow borders
🌺 Perennial

Peonies

Lush, fragrant, and long-lived — a peony plant can bloom for 50+ years with minimal care. A garden heirloom.

🌱 Growing Guide
  • Hardiness: Zones 3–8 (need cold winters to bloom)
  • Planting: Plant eyes no more than 1–2 inches deep
  • Ants on buds are harmless — they feed on nectar
  • Support heavy blooms with peony rings
  • Cut back foliage to the ground in fall
🍃 Shade Perennial

Hostas

The king of shade gardens — bold foliage in every shade of green, blue, and gold. Virtually indestructible.

🌱 Growing Guide
  • Hardiness: Zones 3–9
  • Sun: Part to full shade
  • Soil: Rich, moist, well-drained
  • Slugs are the main pest — use DE or copper tape
  • Divide clumps in spring every 3–5 years for free plants
🔵 Perennial

Salvia (Perennial Sage)

Drought-tolerant spires of violet-blue that hummingbirds and bees absolutely adore — blooms until frost.

🌱 Growing Guide
  • Hardiness: Zones 4–9
  • Sun: Full sun
  • Soil: Well-drained; tolerates poor soil
  • Bloom time: May through frost with deadheading
  • Best varieties: May Night, Blue Hill, Caradonna

Intentional Planting

What is Intentional Planting? It's designing your garden with purpose — choosing plants that work together to protect each other, attract beneficial insects, repel pests, and actively build soil health. This section guides you through proven companion planting pairs and cover crop strategies.
🤝

Companion Planting Pairs

🌼 Companion Pair
🍅 Tomatoes + 🌼 Marigolds
Repels Nematodes Deters Whitefly Attracts Bees

Marigolds release a chemical from their roots that repels root-knot nematodes. Plant French marigolds densely around the tomato bed perimeter for best effect.

🌱 How to Plant This Pair
  • Use French marigolds (Tagetes patula) — most effective variety
  • Plant 6–12" apart in a dense border around tomato beds
  • Interplant between tomato cages for whitefly protection
  • Let marigolds bloom all season — don't deadhead completely
  • Works best with at least 2 full growing seasons to suppress nematodes

💡 Bonus Benefit Marigold flowers attract hoverflies, whose larvae eat aphids at rates of up to 400 per day.
🌿 Companion Pair
🫑 Peppers + 🌿 Basil
Repels Aphids Deters Spider Mites Improves Flavor

The volatile oils in basil confuse and repel aphids and spider mites. Many gardeners report improved pepper flavor from proximity planting — worth trying!

🌱 How to Plant This Pair
  • Plant basil 12–18" from each pepper plant
  • Interplant rows: one row peppers, one row basil, alternating
  • Allow basil to flower — the blooms attract beneficial wasps
  • Harvest basil regularly to keep it bushy and aromatic
  • Works with sweet, hot, and bell pepper varieties
🧡 Companion Pair
🥒 Cucumbers + 🧡 Nasturtiums
Trap Crop Repels Beetles Edible Flowers

Nasturtiums act as a magnet for aphids, luring them away from cucumbers. They also repel cucumber beetles and squash bugs — and the flowers are edible!

🌱 How to Plant This Pair
  • Plant nasturtiums at the ends and corners of cucumber rows
  • Let them sprawl freely — they need no support
  • When aphids colonize nasturtiums, pull and compost those plants
  • Direct sow nasturtium seeds after last frost — they resent transplanting
  • Trailing variety recommended: 'Alaska', 'Jewel Mix'
🥕 Companion Pair
🥦 Brassicas + 🌿 Dill
Repels Cabbage Moth Attracts Predatory Wasps Beneficial Insect Hub

Dill's umbrella flowers attract tiny parasitic wasps that prey on cabbage loopers and imported cabbageworms — natural biological control at its finest.

🌱 How to Plant This Pair
  • Sow dill seeds between cabbage, kale, and broccoli rows
  • Allow dill to bolt and flower — that's when it does its best work
  • Plant in succession every 3 weeks to keep flowers coming
  • ⚠️ Keep dill away from carrots — they cross-pollinate and degrade flavor
  • Also pairs well with: lettuce, onion, cucumbers
⭐ Companion Pair
🍓 Strawberries + ⭐ Borage
Repels Hornworms Boosts Yield Attracts Pollinators

Borage is a powerhouse companion — it repels tomato hornworms and strawberry pests, attracts bees to boost pollination, and its leaves add trace minerals to the soil.

🌱 How to Plant This Pair
  • Direct sow borage seeds between strawberry plants in early spring
  • Borage is a self-seeder — plant once, enjoy for years
  • Edible blue star flowers can be used in salads & drinks
  • Deadhead selectively to control spread
  • Also excellent companion for: tomatoes, squash, and beans
💜 Companion Pair
🥬 Kale / Cabbage + 💜 Lavender
Repels Cabbage White Deters Aphids Fragrant Border

The strong camphor scent of lavender disrupts the navigation of cabbage white butterflies and aphids, reducing egg-laying on your brassica crops significantly.

🌱 How to Plant This Pair
  • Plant lavender as a permanent border around rotating brassica beds
  • Use compact varieties like 'Hidcote' or 'Munstead' for tight spaces
  • Space lavender 18" apart to form a dense aromatic hedge
  • Cut lavender bunches and hang inside the coop too — repels mites!

Cover Crops for Natural Soil Building

Why Cover Crops? After harvest, bare soil loses nutrients, erodes, and invites weeds. Cover crops protect and actively improve your soil — fixing nitrogen, adding organic matter, suppressing weeds, and breaking pest cycles naturally.
🍀 Cover Crop

Crimson Clover

One of the best nitrogen-fixing cover crops for the homestead garden — produces up to 100–150 lbs N/acre and gorgeous crimson blooms that attract pollinators.

🌱 How to Use It
  • When to sow: Early fall (6 wks before frost) or early spring
  • Seeding rate: 8–10 lbs per 1,000 sq ft; broadcast and rake in
  • Terminate: Mow or till in 2–3 weeks before planting next crop
  • Best for: Beds that will grow tomatoes, corn, or squash next
  • Let it flower briefly first — pollinators will thank you

🌾 Cover Crop

Winter Rye

The hardiest of all cover crops — germinates in near-freezing temperatures and produces massive biomass to smother weeds and feed soil microbes in spring.

🌱 How to Use It
  • When to sow: Fall, right up until the first hard frost
  • Seeding rate: 2–3 lbs per 1,000 sq ft; broadcast and rake in
  • Terminate: Mow or till in spring, 2–3 weeks before planting
  • Best for: Beds that will grow heavy feeders (tomatoes, peppers, squash)
  • Winter rye's deep root system breaks compacted soil
